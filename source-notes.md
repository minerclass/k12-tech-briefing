# E-Rate update and source collection


Status: Public source reference. Snapshot: October 21, 2026. Verify current developments before use.

## Current assessment

E-Rate faces separate legal and policy questions. The court case concerns the authority to create its administrator. The FCC proceedings concern program scope and administration. Neither the newsletter nor the sources reviewed supplies a reason for applicants to abandon current filing work.

### Court ruling

On October 15, a Fifth Circuit panel held that the FCC created USAC without the specific congressional authorization required by the Government Corporation Control Act. The remedy vacated the fourth-quarter 2025 USF contribution factor and remanded to the FCC. This concerns the funding mechanism across USF programs, rather than an E-Rate application denial. The opinion does not direct USAC to stop processing applications or invoices. See the [court opinion, especially pages 1 and 19–20](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/25/25-60535-CV0.pdf).

Funds For Learning reports that the ruling was not yet effective and that the FCC was considering its options. Its October 20 explanation recommends continuing FY2026 invoicing and FY2027 planning. That is a dated operational interpretation, not a guarantee against later disruption. [FFL explanation](https://www.fundsforlearning.com/news/fifth-circuit-rules-the-fcc-lacked-authority-to-create-usac-heres-what-it-means-for-e-rate/).

The Supreme Court's June 27, 2025 decision rejected the earlier nondelegation challenge to the contribution structure. The 2026 panel addressed a different statutory issue. Avoid suggesting the Fifth Circuit overruled the Supreme Court. [Supreme Court opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/24pdf/24-354_0861.pdf).

### FCC review

FCC 26-41 asks whether to narrow or sunset E-Rate, reconsider supported services and discounts, restrict its geographic reach, broaden CIPA interpretations, address screen time, and regulate consultants. These are questions and proposals, not adopted restrictions. Replies are due November 12 in WC 26-133 and the associated dockets 13-184, 21-93, and 21-455. [Official Federal Register notice](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-14/pdf/2026-16590.pdf).

A separate proceeding, FCC 26-52 / WC 26-173, examines USAC processes, oversight, structure, operating costs, and board governance. Its reply deadline is October 30. This proceeding concerns all USF programs. [Official Federal Register notice](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-31/pdf/2026-17761.pdf).

### Congressional and Illinois responses

The October 15 congressional letter urges preserving broad eligibility and distinguishes connectivity support from classroom device policies. It is advocacy, not a change in law. The organizing release identifies Democratic caucus participation and independents; do not call this letter bipartisan without additional evidence. Illinois Senators Tammy Duckworth and Dick Durbin signed. [Signed letter](https://www.markey.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/letter_to_chairman_carr_on_e-rate_rulemaking.pdf), [organizing release](https://www.markey.senate.gov/news/press-releases/sen-markey-leader-schumer-reps-matsui-menendez-meng-lead-colleagues-in-demanding-trump-fcc-withdraw-proposal-to-undermine-e-rate-program).

Funds For Learning reports 111 signers, including 46 senators and 65 House members. Attribute that count to its coverage if used; signatures were not independently recounted. [FFL coverage](https://www.fundsforlearning.com/news/111-members-of-congress-urge-fcc-to-keep-e-rate-open-to-all-schools-and-libraries/).

Illinois Attorney General Kwame Raoul joined a coalition of 23 attorneys general advocating preservation. His office reports that Illinois received more than $94 million annually during the past five years. Attribute the figure to that office; it was not independently recalculated from USAC records. Its legal objections are the coalition's position. [October 14 Illinois release](https://www.illinoisattorneygeneral.gov/news/story/attorney-general-raoul-takes-action-to-support-internet-for-schools-and-libraries-as-fcc-considers-cuts).

## Applicant calendar and practical implications

| Date | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| October 21 | FY2027 EPC administrative window opens. Review profile information before it locks ahead of the 2027 Form 471 window. |
| October 28 | Usual FY2025 recurring-service invoice deadline; confirm each FRN's actual date. USAC describes a 15-day window for extension requests under the current rule, not extra time for the invoice itself. |
| October 29 | FY2026 Form 486 deadline for commitments issued on or before July 1 with a July 1 service start. |
| October 30 | Replies in WC 26-173 on USF administration. |
| November 12 | Replies in WC 26-133 and associated E-Rate review dockets. |

The first three items come from [USAC's current announcements](https://www.usac.org/e-rate/resources/announcements/); the reply dates come from the two official notices above. Do not apply the invoice or Form 486 dates universally. USAC also reports that SPI and BEAR filers need active SAM.gov registration and a UEI on Form 498 for disbursements. Review that separately if payments are delayed.

For state master contracts, determine whether the application cites the state's Form 470 or the applicant's own. Multiple-award arrangements generally require evaluating every contracted provider capable of serving the applicant, with eligible price weighted most heavily. USAC describes an exception when only one provider can supply service. A purchasing vehicle alone does not establish compliance. [USAC state master contract guidance](https://www.usac.org/e-rate/applicant-process/selecting-service-providers/state-master-contracts/).

## Verification needed immediately before publication

- Check the appellate docket for rehearing, a stay, mandate, or subsequent order. No full live docket audit was completed here; the reviewed sources do not establish later procedural developments.
- Check for FCC deadline extensions and any final action. Neither open NPRM should be described as an adopted termination or rural-only rule.
- Recheck the operational pages for the relevant funding year and individual FRNs.
- If quoting local financial effects, use authorized public figures, label commitments versus payments, and show the year. No district-specific funding analysis was performed.
- If using the 98.6% filing figure, retain its denominator, attribution, and limitations. It does not establish national public opinion.
- If using the 62% increase, call it growth in requests over the stated comparison years. Do not convert it into a claim about actual spending or demonstrated educational benefits.
- Confirm current eligible-service definitions before making broader claims about cybersecurity, off-premises connectivity, or the new competitive-bidding portal. This brief does not audit those entire rulesets.
